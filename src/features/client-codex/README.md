# The client codex — Notes & Profile as seven pages

Client codex round, Sep 2026 (`docs/rounds/2026-09-24-client-codex.md`). The profile's Notes & Profile tab stops being one long scroll and becomes an Overview and six pages — **Overview · Notes · FORD · Body & Pulse · Goals & Focus · Story · Account** — switched by the profile's own sub-toggle. Where the trainer is lives in the profile's one navigation reducer (`src/features/client-profile/profile-nav.ts`: `RecordPage`, `RECORD_ANCHORS`, `neighbours()`).

This folder is built phase by phase on the `client-codex` branch. It holds **the kit** (phase 5), **the shell** (phase 8) and **Body & Pulse** (`body/`, phases 12–13); each page area adds its section here as it rebuilds its page.

## The shell (`ClientCodex.tsx`)

`ClientProfileView` mounts `ClientCodex` on the record tab, keyed on the client, in a panel it keeps mounted after the tab's first visit (the All Machines and Trends precedent), so coming back re-reads nothing and an unsaved edit survives a trip to Journey.

The rules the shell keeps:

- **The Overview mounts with the tab; every other page mounts on its first visit and then stays, hidden.** A page's own reads (Goals' shared notes and jots, Body's Pulse draft) cost nothing until someone opens it; switching back costs no fetch; a composer, an open editor or an unsaved edit survives a page switch. Every panel is in the DOM from the start (empty until visited), so the sub-toggle's `aria-controls` always points at one. The render test counts the listeners: one each on the journal, FORD and the scans, whatever is visited.
- **No `autoFocus` inside a page.** Pages stay mounted while hidden, so an autofocus would steal focus from whatever page is showing. Focus moves only on an action: a neighbour, a Next card or Show moves it to the new page's title.
- **A door to a card scrolls it under the sticky bar** (`scroll-margin-top` from `--psub-stuck-h`); a page change with no card puts the page's top under the bar only if the bar is stuck, and never yanks the header away. Notes' own anchors (`notes-*`, `note-{id}`) are Notes' to interpret: the shell does not scroll to them but hands them to the Notes page as a one-shot request keyed by the move (`client-notes/notes-intent.ts`), and the page opens the thread, the composer or Resolved first, then brings it into view. Everything in the layout effect is guarded — `scrollIntoView` is feature-detected, and nothing may throw.
- **Anything that measures its own width uses a (feature-detected) ResizeObserver.** A hidden page is `display: none` and measures 0 until it is shown.
- **The critical line** (Notes' `CriticalLine`) sits under the bar on FORD, Body & Pulse, Goals & Focus, Story and Account; the Overview and Notes draw the critical note themselves.
- **One Save bar, one write.** Mounted once (its live region must exist before the first change), drawn only while something is unsaved and only for a reader who may edit. It says how many changes and where ("1 unsaved change · FORD · Occupation"); Show goes back, Discard reverts, Save writes the changed fields in one `updateDoc`. A refused save keeps every edit and says where the record is kept. The form re-seeds from the record only while nothing is unsaved.
- **Read only is a mirror of the rules, not a guess.** A reader who may not edit (a cross-train studio, a franchise owner who does not work at the home studio) gets the context line "Read only here · {home} keeps this record. Notes you write still save.", the record's editors locked (`pages/RecordLock.tsx`, a disabled fieldset — interim, until each page's read views), no Save bar, no Migration Hub, and no FORD listener. The form is the backstop: `useRecordForm({ canEdit: false })` takes no edit and saves nothing, so a control that escapes the lock can never stage a change nobody can see.
- **`FIELD_HOME` stays total.** A new editable field needs a key in `RECORD_FORM_KEYS` and a home on a card whose anchor is in `RECORD_ANCHORS`; `record-form.test.ts` fails otherwise. A key that is not a form key is refused by `updateField`, so a retired field (`recoveryMetric`) or a Mindbody one can never be written from here.

**Notes are Notes'.** What any page says about a client's notes — how to coach her, injury threads, her notes on each machine, older life notes by pillar, the FORD door's count, the Notes segment's line — comes from `src/features/client-notes/record-selectors.ts`, over the tab's one journal load, and the red line under the bar is Notes' `CriticalLine`. The kit builds no second critical strip and no page writes its own note selection.

**One load, and the page's own reads.** The journal, the Pulse history and the InBody scans are the tab's one load. The page's own reads happen once, on its first visit: **the client's ONE Pulse draft** (`useCheckInDraft`, handed to the editor through its `draft` prop — never a second one: two drafts of one client autosave side by side), the machine catalog (`useMachineCatalog`, the same collection the app already streams) and machine fit's reads for "clients built like her" — the same cached studio index (`FRESH_MS`) and company trend reads as Programming → Setup, with the same stored "Similar to" spec (`machine-fit/ui/stored-spec.ts`), and **none at all for a client with no height**, who cannot be compared. What Programming holds (her settings, routines, the roster, the studio) comes from the profile as `CodexData.programming` — no read of the tab's own — with a `status`: until her routines, her settings and the catalog have all answered, On our floor claims nothing that depends on them (no "standard set-up" count, no "Not in her routines", no "N of M machines set up") and says which read is out (`floorReads`, floor.ts).

**The Pulse on this page is two halves read together** (`pulse-read.ts`): the open draft wins where it has an answer; otherwise the newest saved round that asked, from the tab's one read-only history (the living rule). A failed history says "couldn't be loaded", never "Not asked yet" — on every card that reads it: the Pulse card, Measured and told (`toldMissingLine`, pairs.ts: "Loading what she told us…" while the saved rounds or the open round are out) and Where it matters.

**The app describes; the trainer decides.** No load, no weight to use, no body-type label, no progression anywhere on the page; the post-session dose's "next time" wording is deliberately not reused. The "Recovery between sessions" select is gone (decision 7).

**Over time costs no read** (phase 13). The sessions are the journal's own: `useClientJournal` already streams her `SESSION_SUMMARY_LIMIT` (40) newest session documents for the wrap-up notes and hands them out as `recentSessions` — there is no sessions query anywhere on the tab (the shell's render test holds it). `arrivals.ts` reads each completed session through `rating/session-reads.ts` (`readinessDial`, `doseOf`, `regionDial`, so a legacy `sleepQuality: "poor"` sits on the same axis as a Dial tap), and the figure's region list gets the door's taps from it ("At the door: “Stiff” on Mar 20 · tapped at 3 of her last 12 sessions."). The rules it keeps:

- **Not asked is not "As usual".** An untouched dial is null: it is counted as not asked and draws no mark. A client with no sessions reads "Not asked yet: Journey holds no session of hers in these six months." A line needs `RULE_OF_THREE` answers; below it the sentence says "not enough for a line yet (needs 3)". Pulse lanes need two rounds to draw a line.
- **Her last N sessions, never all.** The listener orders by the `date` string, so a full page may stop short of six months; `pageReach` says since when the count holds ("(since Mar 3)"), and a page crowded with old "9/1/2024"-style dates draws nothing rather than guess which sessions are recent.
- **Only a session the briefing ran for is ever "not asked".** `originOf` tells a session run in Journey (it has a start time) from one imported (the legacy markers, or no start time at all — the chart importer writes none even when it matched the trainer) and one logged later (Log past session's 12:00 UTC mark), and `couldAsk` also leaves out, for recovery, the sessions before `RECOVERY_ASKED_FROM` (Sep 16 2026, `rating/scales.ts` — the question has no legacy field). Those sessions are left out of the count and named for what they are — "asked at 2 of her 3 sessions run in Journey since the question was added (Sep 16)… 1 more was imported, and imports don't record the door." — never "wasn't asked" (the migration rule: an empty Journey history is "no detail here"). A session that DID answer always counts. The figure's door count (`regionTaps`) uses the same rule ("of her last 5 sessions run in Journey").
- **Unknown is a row, not an empty lane.** A loading or failed sessions read replaces the door and the dose with one row ("Her recent sessions couldn't be loaded just now… The rest of this page is unaffected." — no Try again: the listener's error is final), and the Pulse and InBody still draw; a loading or failed Pulse or InBody read is its own row. The row says the state once: the lede meanwhile says only what the card is about. The footer's counts leave out whatever is unknown, never 0, and they are Journey's ("… and 0 sessions in Journey in these six months"), not her whole six months.
- **"No Pulse answer saved" only when it is true.** The timeline draws three statements and two pain spots, and Update Pulse answers one area at a time, so a round in the window that answered other areas is common: that row reads "1 saved Pulse round in these six months, with no answer to the statements drawn here; the Pulse card below has every area." "No Pulse answer saved in these six months." is said only when no saved round falls in the window and the history read reaches its first day (a truncated history says "since …").
- **The app describes.** `doseSentence` (lib/post-session) is not reused: its "next time" clauses are progression talk. No score, no percentage, no colour by severity — marks below the centre are ink, the rest muted ink.
- **It measures itself safely.** `BodyTimeline` draws at the container's width, measured in a layout effect with a feature-detected `ResizeObserver` (640 when there is none), and each lane's title and sentence are HTML over its SVG strip, so a long question wraps at portrait widths instead of being cut off at the drawing's edge. `BodyTimeline.render.test.tsx` mounts it with no `ResizeObserver`.

## The kit (`kit/`)

One look for every page, so a trainer learns it once. An area's own stylesheet may lay out its own content; it may not draw a second panel, button, chip or eyebrow.

| Piece | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| `Page` | A whole page: `PageHead` (the 30px title, the lede, the page's own actions, the ‹ › neighbour buttons) + the content + `NextCard` ("Next: Body & Pulse", or "Done" from Account back to the Overview). The Overview is not a `Page`: no head, no neighbours, no Next card. |
| `Card`, `CardHead` | THE panel. `id` takes an anchor from `RECORD_ANCHORS` and adds `data-cx-anchor`. `host` is the frame for a component that already draws its own cards (spacing only, so borders never double). |
| `Slot` | An Overview tile. Either the whole tile is one door (`as="button"`, 120px or taller, holding no other control) or it is a section whose rows are doors. |
| `FactList`, `Fact` | Label and value; one column when the panel is narrower than 480px. |
| `Rows`, `Row` | A list of lines that open something: a label column, the text, meta — stacked when the list is narrow. 44px or taller. |
| `Chip`, `ChipButton`, `LoudChip` | A label that wraps (26px, not tappable); a tappable chip (40px); a note's loudness in the words and colours of the one Loudness control (`LOUDNESS_TONE`: Note quiet, Heads up plum, Critical crimson). |
| `Eyebrow`, `Meta`, `Lede`, `Quote`, `Source`, `SectionHead`, `BigNumber`, `EmptyLine` | The words, at 11, 12, 17, 14, 12, 17, 30 and 14px. `Quote` takes a string and puts it in curly quotes verbatim — for anything the page quotes rather than says (a watch-out, a Pulse statement). `EmptyLine` is for "nothing on file", never for "couldn't load". |
| `Btn` | The one button: default, `solid` (brand blue — never hero orange, which is Start Session's), `live`, `quiet`. 40px or taller; the words wrap rather than clip. |
| `FordMark` | FORD's letter in its pillar's tint — identity only, never a status. |
| `ReadEdit`, `useReadEdit`, `EditButton` | Read view first, Edit on demand. The editor writes to the shell's record form; "Done" only closes it, the edits wait for the Save bar, and the card says "Unsaved" meanwhile. Every editor closes when the form's `revision` changes (a save or a discard). A reader who may not edit gets no Edit button. |
| `TextInput`, `TextArea`, `SelectInput`, `Picks`, `MultiPicks`, `Pick` | The inputs: 14px, a 12px label, 40px or taller. A stored value the options no longer offer is shown and kept, never silently replaced. The `<label>` holds only the label's words (the control's name); the hint is its description (`aria-describedby`), so VoiceOver reads each once. |
| `SaveBar` | The one Save bar: "1 unsaved change · FORD · Occupation", with Show, Discard and Save changes. Sticky to the bottom of the profile's scroller (`useScrollerPad`), opaque, and no bar is drawn while nothing is unsaved. `saveBarSentence` (`save-bar.ts`) is its words. The announcement is a visually hidden live region beside the bar that is always mounted — a live region that arrives already holding its words is usually not read — so **mount the SaveBar once and pass the count**; don't render it conditionally. |

**Copy helpers** (`kit/text.ts`): `curly`, `cap`, `plural`, `joinDots`, `monthDay`, `monthDayYear`, `dayKeyDate` (a day key at local noon), `inTime`, `monthLabel`, and `firstSentences`, which lives in `src/lib/first-sentences.ts` so Notes can use it without importing from here.

- `inTime(days)` counts days up to 30 ("in 17 days" — FORD's "soon" window), then weeks up to 83 ("in 5 weeks"), then months, then years. **FORD's Coming up uses it with `cap()`** ("In 17 days") rather than a `fromNowLabel` of its own, so the sub-toggle, the Overview and the FORD page say a date one way.
- `firstSentences(text, n)` returns whole sentences until the line holds `n` characters — a note is never cut mid-sentence and never gets an "…" it did not have. The word after a full stop, `?` or `!` decides whether it ended a sentence: a lower-case word or a number means it did not ("7 a.m. only", "her P.T. last week", "Fig. 2", `"does it pinch?" before`), unless the number is a list marker ("Seat at 6. 2. Pad high"). Only before a capital does it need its short abbreviation list ("Dr. Patel"), so an abbreviation it does not know makes the line longer, not a cut instruction. A line break always ends a sentence.

None of these uses a regex lookbehind: older iPadOS Safari fails the whole module when it parses one, and the tests read the files to keep it that way.

**Pronouns** (`kit/pronouns.ts`): the header prints the client's name once; page copy says "she", "he" or "they" through `pronounsOf(client)`, from the gender Mindbody holds (read by machine fit's `parseGender`). Female is she/her, Male is he/his, anything else — no gender, "Other", "None" — is they/their, and `known` is false so a screen can tell a fallback from a fact. Pass both verb forms to `agree(p, "says", "say")`. This is the overnight default for AJ's open question (Sep 24 2026), reversible in that one file.

## The contract, and the test that holds it

`codex.tokens.css` declares the `--cx-*` tokens, and **every colour is an alias of an existing `--eq-*` or `--ford-*` token** — no new palette, light and dark for free. They are declared on `.cx` (the codex's root), not `:root`, so they resolve inside any theme wrapper around it; a kit piece rendered outside that root (a dialog's portal content) needs the `.cx-kit` class to find them.

`kit/scale.test.ts` reads every codex file and fails on:

1. **A text size off the scale** — 11, 12, 14, 17 or 30px (or `var(--cx-fs-*)`, or `inherit`), in CSS (`font-size` and the `font` shorthand) and in components (`fontSize`, a `font:` in a style object, Tailwind `text-sm` / `text-[13px]`).
2. **A raw hex colour** anywhere, the tokens file included — and, in a component, any colour of its own: `rgb()` / `hsl()`, a named or raw value in a style or an SVG `fill` / `stroke` (only `var(--cx-*)`, `currentColor`, `inherit`, `transparent` and `none` pass), or a Tailwind colour utility (`text-red-500`, `bg-white`). A component prop that is not a colour is called `tone`, never `color`.
3. **Clipped text** — `text-overflow: ellipsis`; a one-line cut-off, whether `nowrap` and `overflow: hidden` share a rule, are split across two rules for the same selector, or come as Tailwind classes (`whitespace-nowrap` with `overflow-hidden`) or a style object; Tailwind `truncate`. The one exception: a note or beat BODY in a list row may clamp to exactly two lines, because the whole note is one tap away, and only in the exactly named body selectors (`LINE_CLAMP_BODIES`: `.nx-row__body`, `.gf-row__text` — the Notes and Goals phases confirm the names when they build their rows). A row's name, machine name, label or meta can never be clamped.
4. **A regex lookbehind** in code that ships to an iPad.
5. **A raw invisible or control character** (a no-break space, a zero-width space, a byte-order mark) in any codex source or test file — write the escape.

`CODEX_FILES` is an explicit list, and a file in this folder that is not on it fails the suite, so the folder cannot grow a file the scan never reads. Every page area's phase adds its files (including its files in other folders) — Notes' critical line is the first: `client-notes/CriticalLine.tsx`, `critical-line.css` and `record-selectors.ts` are on the list, because the shell mounts that line under the bar on five pages. `HOSTED_FILES` and `HOSTED_OFF_SCALE_BUDGET` are for components the codex mounts but does not own yet: their off-scale text sizes are counted against a budget instead of failing (168 when the shell landed, phase 8 — the long scroll's sections in `pages/legacy-blocks.tsx` and the pieces the pages host directly). A file may be on one list or the other, never both; each area phase takes its pieces off the hosted list and lowers the budget to the new count. The Notes page (phase 9) put its page, catalog, card, row, `notes-page.css` and `notes-intent.ts` on `CODEX_FILES`; the composer and the To-file tray it mounts are shared with the session sheet and keep their sizes there, so on the page `.nx-notes` rules bring every off-scale rule of `notes.css` onto the scale — and a test fails if one is missing an override (the shared Loudness control keeps its own sizes everywhere; it is one control). The FORD page (phase 10) put `ford/page/*`, `ford-page.css`, FORD's pure modules (`ask-next`, `coming-up`, `pulse-links`, `page-model`) and the life editors (`client-life/LifeBaseline.tsx`, `controls.tsx`, `client-life.css`, `life.ts`) on the list, deleted the FORD hub, and lowered the budget to 148. Body & Pulse (phase 12) put `body/*` and the InBody card (`inbody/InBodyCard.tsx`, `inbody-card.css`) on the list, deleted the watch-out banner, took the Pulse area out of `ClientJournalTab`, collapsed the flag picker's stylesheet onto the scale, and lowered the budget to 125 (the picker stays hosted: its screen-reader-only text is clipped on purpose). It also checks the tokens: exactly the five sizes, every colour an alias of a declared app token, touch targets at 40px or more, and `kit.css` drawing only from `--cx-*`.

`kit/kit.render.test.tsx` mounts every primitive in light and dark, and asserts that every control the kit renders matches a `kit.css` rule that makes it at least 40px tall.

To prove the scale test bites: add `.x { font-size: 13px; }` to `kit.css`, run `TZ=America/New_York npx vitest run --dir src client-codex/kit/scale`, watch it fail, and take the line out.
