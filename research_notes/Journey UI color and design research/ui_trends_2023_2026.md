# UI design 2023-2026: what changed, what is substance, and what transfers to Journey (a trainer's iPad on the gym floor and a studio leader's dashboard)

*Method note for the report writer: researched Oct 8 2026. This environment's egress proxy refused direct page fetches for most sites (nngroup.com, design.google, wikipedia.org, macrumors.com, mjtsai.com all failed), so every finding below comes from the search tool's excerpts of the cited page, not from a full read. Where an excerpt was thin, or the source is secondary, or sources conflict, that is said on the line. Anything dated before 2023 is marked BACKGROUND. "Inferences" are this researcher's reasoning applied to Journey, not claims from the sources.*

## 1. Apple's Liquid Glass (WWDC June 2025, iOS/iPadOS 26): what it is, the HIG changes, the reception, Apple's later adjustments, and what it means for a web app on an iPad

### Takeaway
Liquid Glass is a translucent, lensing, adaptive material for the navigation/control layer, inspired by visionOS. Its legibility was widely criticised: NN/g, Oct 10 2025, "Liquid Glass Is Cracked, and Usability Suffers in iOS 26". Apple retreated step by step. iOS 26.1 (Nov 2025) added a Clear/Tinted choice. The design chief Alan Dye left for Meta in Dec 2025. iOS 27 (announced June 2026) retuned the material and added a clear-to-tinted slider. For an iPad web app, the substance worth taking is cheap and needs no glass: concentric corner radii, a calm scroll-edge treatment under sticky bars, and "glass only on chrome, never on content". The glass itself is costly or impossible in Safari. Safari supports blur, but the lensing displacement does not render. Safari also does not expose Reduce Transparency to CSS. Blur on sticky bars re-composites on every scroll frame, which is the wrong trade for a 10th-generation iPad on the gym floor.

### Cited Findings
**What it is (Apple's own framing, WWDC25)**
- In "Meet Liquid Glass" (WWDC25 session 219), Apple describes objects that "materialize in and out by gradually modulating the light bending and lensing". The material is "designed from the ground up to be adaptive to both its size and its environment". Its "primary goal is to remain visually clear, deferring to the content underneath", while "constantly, subtly changing to ensure legibility and to maintain clear separation from the content layer". Liquid Glass "dynamically morphs between the controls in each context", which "maintains the concept of having a singular floating plane" — [Apple WWDC25 "Meet Liquid Glass"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Concentricity: buttons, switches, media controls, tab bars and sidebars were reworked to be concentric with the rounded corners of the hardware and app windows — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2807925/meet-liquid-glass-apple-redesigns-all-its-interfaces-at-once.html). A developer guide (secondary) states the rule: a nested radius equals the parent radius minus the padding. Capsules are preferred for most interactive elements on iOS/iPadOS, and SwiftUI exposes a concentric-shape modifier — [dev.to summary of WWDC25](https://dev.to/arshtechpro/wwdc-2025-apples-liquid-glass-design-system-52an)
- A commentator notes concentricity is applied unevenly even inside Apple's own apps. Terminal and System Information windows have a much smaller corner radius — [Pixel Envy linklog on HIG update](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/hig-liquid-glass/)
- Craig Federighi: "the most obvious inspiration was visionOS". Glass lets an interface "sit in the context" while the frame takes less visual space — [TechRadar interview](https://www.techradar.com/phones/ios/we-did-all-this-work-with-physical-glass-simulating-as-closely-as-we-could-the-actual-properties-of-glass-for-liquid-glass-says-apples-craig-federighi-then-they-went-further)

**How the HIG changed**
- The updated HIG is framed around three principles: Hierarchy, Harmony and Consistency. The companion session is "Get to know the new design system" — [Apple WWDC25 session 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/); principle names via [Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/hig-liquid-glass/)
- In early September 2025 Apple refreshed the materials section of the HIG. It covers semantic use, text legibility, contrast, and avoiding stacked glass. "Regular" glass is for text-heavy surfaces (alerts, sidebars) and "Clear" glass for visually rich backgrounds (photos, video). Liquid Glass belongs mainly in navigation and controls, "not behind ordinary content or in stacked layers of glass" — [Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/hig-liquid-glass/) (secondary summary of the HIG; the HIG page itself was not reachable)
- Floating, shrinking tab bars: on iPhone the tab bar "floats above the content, and can be configured to minimize on scroll" — [Apple WWDC25 session 284](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/). The API `tabBarMinimizeBehavior` has `.automatic`, `.never`, `.onScrollDown` and `.onScrollUp`. `.automatic` does NOT minimize on iPadOS, and the directional values apply only on iPhone. A minimized bar shows only the selected tab's icon. A `tabViewBottomAccessory` gets a glass capsule automatically and moves inline beside the minimized bar — [Donny Wals](https://www.donnywals.com/exploring-tab-bars-on-ios-26-with-liquid-glass/); [Create with Swift](https://www.createwithswift.com/enhancing-the-tab-bar-with-a-bottom-accessory/)

**Reception: legibility and usability**
- NN/g (Raluca Budiu, Oct 10 2025): "iOS 26's visual language obscures content instead of letting it take the spotlight". Under "Crowded, Smaller Tap Targets", tab bars "feel cramped, squeezed to make room for the ever present search button". The bar collapses when search activates (Summary and Sharing shrink into a single icon). "In iOS 26, controls appear, vanish, collapse, and expand depending on context". Overall verdict: Apple prioritises spectacle over usability — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/) (via search excerpts and reposts: [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45544044), [Roger Wong](https://rogerwong.me/2025/10/liquid-glass-is-cracked-and-usability-suffers-in-ios-26), [OSnews](https://www.osnews.com/story/143522/liquid-glass-is-cracked-and-usability-suffers-in-ios-26/))
- A Spanish-language summary of NN/g says the transparency creates a "foggy window" effect, and that overlapping translucent layers over complex backgrounds noticeably reduce contrast — [ecosistemastartup (unverified against the original)](https://ecosistemastartup.com/?p=47383)
- Fast Company reported that every UX specialist it spoke to flagged legibility. Some said Liquid Glass adds cognitive load and creates accessibility problems absent from earlier versions — [Fast Company (ME edition)](https://fastcompanyme.com/?p=41660)
- On principle: "As the opacity of the panel's background drops, so does the contrast whenever it appears over anything else that is not itself entirely white" — [Pixel Envy, "On Liquid Glass"](https://pxlnv.com/blog/on-liquid-glass/)
- UX Collective calls Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast "optional patches" rather than design foundations. It also notes notification text distorting over high-contrast backgrounds — [uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/liquid-glass-is-beautiful-but-is-the-chemistry-missing-00b4f79ff529)
- Hacker News commenters said iOS 26's "compact" tab mode relies on undiscoverable gestures. Switching back to the "bottom" tab mode in Settings restores something close to the old layout — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45544044) (anecdotal)
- iPad: a reviewer calls iPadOS 26 "great" overall. On the home screen, though, "the transparency goes too far and the background icons blend into the foreground icons" — [Andrew Owen](https://andrewowen.net/blog/ipados-26-is-great-liquid-glass-not-so-much/)

**Reception: performance and battery (anecdotal or press, not controlled)**
- Reports of jittery visuals, choppy scrolling and sluggishness on older iPhones. Reviewers attribute this to real-time rendering of the glass on older A-series chips — [BGR](https://bgr.com/2047698/how-to-fix-ios-26-slowing-down-iphone-guide/); "Performance on older phones is hit or miss" — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-26-complaints-are-piling-up-should-you-wait-to-upgrade)
- Battery tests reportedly showed iOS 26 using more energy than iOS 18 for regular tasks — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1982231/why-ios-26-is-draining-your-iphones-battery). Macworld compared the power draw to 3D gaming — [Macworld](https://macworld.com/article/3129163/i-cant-wait-for-ios-27-to-fix-my-iphone.html). Counterpoint: on an iPhone 16 Pro, John Gruber found iOS 18 felt slower than iOS 26 because animations were sped up late in the beta. That says nothing about older devices — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3036875/ios-26-update-liquid-glass-performance-features-security.html) (as relayed in search coverage; the Daring Fireball original was not retrieved)

**Apple's adjustments, in order**
- iOS/iPadOS 26.1 (released early Nov 2025) added Settings > Display & Brightness > Liquid Glass with "Clear" (default) and "Tinted". Tinted "increases opacity of the material in apps and notifications on the Lock Screen". It sits in addition to Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/03/apple-releases-ios-26-1-for-iphone-with-these-changes/); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2947565/ios-26-1-beta-4-does-the-unthinkable-you-can-control-how-glassy-liquid-glass-should-be.html); the iPadOS 26.1 release-note wording for the 10th-gen iPad is via [ipsw.me](https://ipsw.me/download/iPad13,18/23B85/)
- iOS 26.2 added a transparency slider for the Lock Screen clock — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/12/ios-27-fixes-liquid-glass-and-not-just-with-a-slider/)
- Personnel: in early Dec 2025 Bloomberg reported, and Apple confirmed, that Alan Dye (VP of Human Interface Design, who fronted Liquid Glass) was leaving for Meta. Stephen Lemay replaced him. Six Colors called Liquid Glass "fairly divisive" — [Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2025/12/in-a-major-coup-for-someone-alan-dye-leaves-apple/); [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/bloomberg-reports-that-alan-dye-is-leaving-apple/)
- iOS 27 (WWDC26, June 2026, beta-period coverage):
  - The material was retuned to "more effectively diffuse complex content".
  - Glass elements got a darkened edge and brighter specular highlights.
  - A "uniform toolbar" appears at the top when content scrolls under the floating bars. It is automatic for standard toolbars and tunable through the scroll-edge-effect APIs.
  - Existing apps inherit these fixes without being recompiled.
  - A Settings slider runs from fully clear to fully tinted. It is also offered during setup, and on iPad it comes with a preview — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/12/ios-27-fixes-liquid-glass-and-not-just-with-a-slider/)
  - Liquid Glass "isn't going anywhere" — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/ios/liquid-glass-isnt-going-anywhere-in-ios-27-but-theres-good-news-for-its-readability)
- An unconfirmed report claimed iOS 27 would move "away from high-refraction backgrounds to solid, high-contrast containers" — [Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-liquid-design-broke-the-iphone-and-forced-apples-great-reset/). What Apple actually announced was a retune plus a slider, not a return to solid containers (per MacRumors and 9to5Mac above).

**Imitating it on the web (Safari on iPad)**
- `backdrop-filter` is supported unprefixed from Safari 18, and with the `-webkit-` prefix before that — [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/backdrop-filter)
- Apple's WebKit engineers (WWDC 2015, BACKGROUND) explained that a backdrop filter re-renders the region behind the element off-screen: "rendering passes are costly, and so we want to make sure we don't over use them" — [WWDC 2015 session 501 index](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2015/501/)
- Practitioner guidance (2026, secondary, unmeasured): blur forces the browser to re-composite everything behind the element every scroll frame. Keep the radius under about 20px on large surfaces, use at most 3-4 glass layers per viewport, and never animate the blur radius (reportedly drops phones to ~30fps); animate opacity instead — [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/liquid-glass-css-backdrop-filter-recipes-2026). A forum anecdote: a Safari page became responsive again only after a few blurred decorative circles were hidden — [Webflow forum](https://discourse.webflow.com/t/my-site-is-extremely-slow-in-safari-practically-unusable/167272)
- The "lensing" part cannot be done in Safari. An SVG `feDisplacementMap` referenced by `backdrop-filter: url(#...)` works in Chromium, but Safari does not render it — [WebKit bug 245510](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245510) (still NEW, with a new test case June 2026). A WebKit contributor said the accelerated filter path used by backdrop-filter cannot run SVG filter references — [WebKit bug 297770](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=297770). Guides treat the effect as "Chrome/Chromium only", with Safari falling back to blur — [Medium/ekino](https://medium.com/ekino-france/liquid-glass-in-css-and-svg-839985fcb88d)
- Safari does not support `@media (prefers-reduced-transparency)` (caniuse lists it unsupported through 26.5, Technology Preview included). The OS setting exists, but CSS cannot see it — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-prefers-reduced-transparency); [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-prefers-reduced-transparency). In June 2025 a CSS Working Group mailing-list post linked Liquid Glass to new contrast problems and asked for this feature — [W3C public-css-archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2025Jun/0328.html)
- Older iPads: the 10th-generation iPad has an A14 Bionic (6-core CPU, 4-core GPU) — [Geekbench device page](https://browser.geekbench.com/ios_devices/ipad-10th-generation); spec line via a low-quality page, [ShopSavvy](https://shopsavvy.com/answers/how-does-the-a14-bionic-chip-perform-in-the-ipad-10th-generation). User-submitted Geekbench 6 results on iPadOS 26.6 show about 2,084 single-core and 4,392 multi-core. An iPad mini (A17 Pro) shows about 2,900-2,976 single-core and about 7,500 multi-core (so roughly 40% / 50-70% faster) — [Geekbench browser](https://browser.geekbench.com/ios_devices/ipad-10th-generation). A 2024 review says the A17 Pro mini's single-core score beat an M1 iPad Pro — [ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/mobile/spv/2410/22/news192_2.html). No source measured Liquid Glass or backdrop-filter cost on either device.

### Inferences
- **Substance vs fashion.**
  - Substance: one floating control plane that defers to content; concentric radii; a scroll-edge treatment that keeps a bar legible when content passes under it; and "glass only on navigation and controls, never stacked". Apple's own 2025-2026 retreat (Tinted, then the darker rim, the uniform toolbar and the slider) shows that legibility over arbitrary content is the hard part, and that the translucency itself is the dispensable part.
  - Fashion: the lensing and refraction, the clear-glass look, and controls that morph or vanish on scroll.
- **For the Active Session**, a screen glanced at twice per machine: NN/g's "controls appear, vanish, collapse, and expand depending on context" is the exact failure mode to avoid. A trainer's eyes must land on the same spot every time. Keep bars fixed and opaque during a session (Journey's Navy Frame already does this).
- **Cheap web-feasible takeaways:**
  - Concentric radii (inner radius = outer radius − padding). This is pure CSS, costs nothing, and suits Journey's raised / well / panel nesting.
  - A solid or gradient "scroll edge" under the sticky header and above the bottom bar, instead of blur.
  - At most, a fixed-radius blur on the frame only (header and bottom bar) as a progressive enhancement over an opaque navy base, never animated, never on cards or the grid, and verified in Journey's perf lab (`harness/perf-lab`) against the iPad-class slowdown.
- **Safari cannot see Reduce Transparency.** Any translucency Journey adds must be opt-in by default or controllable in-app; the opaque style must be the base. This fits Journey's existing opaque "dark fills keep their accent's hue" rule.
- **Do not chase native look-alikes.** A web app imitating iOS 26 glass will look dated or off when iOS 27's tuning differs again (the darker rim, the uniform toolbar). The safest posture is Journey's own coherent look, consistent with the platform's principles (hierarchy, harmony, consistency) rather than its materials.

### Gaps
- The full NN/g article could not be fetched. Its exact recommendations and any test data (participant counts) are unknown; the quotes above come from search excerpts and reposts.
- No measured frame-rate or battery data for `backdrop-filter` on an A14 iPad or the iPad mini was found. Journey's perf lab or Safari Web Inspector on a real 10th-gen iPad would have to supply it.
- The shipped (September 2026) behaviour of iOS 27's Liquid Glass changes was not confirmed. Coverage found was from the June 2026 betas.
- The current text of Apple's HIG Materials and Tab Bars pages was not reachable. The HIG details above come from secondary summaries.

## 2. Google's Material 3 Expressive (May 2025): the research claims, spring motion, shape morphing, emphasised type, and the criticism

### Takeaway
Google calls M3 Expressive its "most-researched update" ever: 46 studies, 18,000+ participants. The famous "up to 4x faster" claim is narrower than it sounds. Eye-tracking showed participants spotted key UI elements up to four times faster across 10 apps. The headline example is a Send button made larger, given a secondary colour and moved above the keyboard. Older participants found key elements as fast as younger ones. All of this is vendor-reported, with no published statistics or independent replication found. The transferable substance is "make the one key action bigger, coloured and near the hand". That is about affordance and placement, not playful style. The spring/shape-morph motion is the part most at odds with a calm, glance-twice tool.

### Cited Findings
- **Scope of the research.** Google tallied "46 separate studies", "hundreds of designs" and "more than 18,000 people from around the world". Methods included eye-tracking, timed tasks and emotional-reaction surveys. A Google blog post published early called it the "most-researched update to Google's design system, ever" — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-material-3-expressive-details-3554486/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91333562/google-maximalist-new-material-design-kit). One secondary source adds "eight countries", which Google's own wording does not confirm — [Synechron](https://www.synechron.com/insight/direction-design-2025)
- **The 4x claim, exact.** "We brought a diverse set of people into a lab with the very latest eye-tracking glasses and had them interact with 10 different apps in both Material 3 Expressive and current Material 3 versions, randomly ordered." Participants "were able to spot key UI elements up to four times faster in the M3 Expressive designs, suggesting that they steer user attention toward the most important part of the screen" — [Google Design, "Expressive Design: Google's UX Research"](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)
  - The example: in the expressive email app the Send button is larger, sits above the keyboard and uses a secondary colour. The baseline has a small Send button in the top toolbar among other controls (same source).
- **Age.** "M3 Expressive designs erased the usability age gap researchers typically see, with older participants spotting key UI elements just as quickly as younger ones". Also: "The time it takes to tap on key actions also significantly decreased" (Google's Cho) — [Dezeen, May 28 2025](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/google-ushers-in-age-of-expressive-interfaces-with-material-design-update/)
- **Preference.** "Strongly preferred by people of all ages", "up to 87%" among 18-to-24-year-olds — [Google Design](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research). Outlets disagree on the age group: Fast Company says Gen Z, Futura says 18-24 — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91333562/google-maximalist-new-material-design-kit)
- **Overstatement in coverage.** Prototypr paraphrased the claim as "4 times faster in the user's journey", but Google limited it to spotting key elements — [Prototypr](https://blog.prototypr.io/googles-new-material-design-expressive-has-me-excited-worried-2cd295758804?gi=945f006e400e)
- **Methodological skepticism (informal).** A Hacker News commenter argued that a circle around the button makes it look more like a button. On that reading, the effect may come from clear affordance rather than expressive styling — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43981861)
- **Timing.** M3 Expressive leaked on May 5 2025 — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/05/05/material-3-expressive-leak/). Google's research post appeared mid-May 2025 (HN thread [43981861](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43981861)), and Dezeen covered the launch on May 28 2025 — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/google-ushers-in-age-of-expressive-interfaces-with-material-design-update/)
- **Motion physics (secondary; the official m3.material.io pages were not retrieved).**
  - Expressive motion replaces cubic-bezier easing with springs set by stiffness and damping. A damping of 1 removes bounce.
  - Motion comes in a "spatial" family (moves and shape morphs such as corner radius) and an "effects" family (opacity and scale), each with fast, default and slow speeds — [Flutter M3EMotion docs](https://pub.dev/documentation/m3e_slider/0.0.2/common_m3e_common/M3EMotion-class.html); [m3x project](https://github.com/tankrap/m3x)
  - Implementation guidance pairs the expressive scheme with hero moments and primary actions, and the calmer standard scheme with "chrome, lists, navigation" — [skills summary (third-party)](https://skills.cat/skills/shelbeely/shelbeely-agent-skills/material-design-3-motion)
  - Android's View-based Material Components added the physics motion system in version 1.13.0 — [Material Components Android Motion.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/theming/Motion.md)
- **Criticism.**
  - Reader comments on Android Authority called it juvenile, mourned lost palette control and mocked the status icons — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/material-3-expressive-dislike-3608927/)
  - Called "infantile" by some — [HTML.it](https://www.html.it/magazine/google-presenta-material-3-expressive-il-nuovo-volto-di-android-16-unestetica-che-ha-gia-subito-critiche/); "feminine" by some — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/googles-new-android-design-language-called-feminine-by-some-has-finally-dropped/)
  - Said to resemble Dribbble/Behance concepts, with doubts about whether long animations and huge elements work in real products — [Habr](https://habr.com/en/articles/913760)
  - Most Android users run manufacturer skins, which limits the reach — [Spidersweb](https://spidersweb.pl/2025/05/googleowi-wyciekl-material-3-expressive.html)
- **Turnaround.** In an Android Authority reader poll a month in, two-thirds said they love it (self-selected) — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/month-with-material-3-expressive-admit-i-was-wrong-3609382/)
- **A later flare-up (Aug 2026).** A Google demo app, "Achieve", applied the language "to an extreme" and drew backlash. The outlet says this was a debate about one demo, not about the system as a whole — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2026/08/19/google-achieve-design-backlash/)

### Inferences
- **The verified, transferable kernel:** "the most important action, made larger, coloured, and placed where the hand already is". This is the same idea as Journey's "orange is now and go" (Start, Finish), and it argues for keeping exactly one loud control per screen near the trainer's thumb. The age-gap finding matters for a staff of mixed ages working at arm's length.
- **Treat the numbers as vendor claims.** "Up to 4x" is a best case over 10 apps, with no published variance and no participant count per study. It should not be quoted to AJ as a general speed-up.
- **Springs and shape-morphing are what Google itself reserves for "hero moments"**; its "standard" scheme is for chrome and lists. On Journey's floor screens, motion should be short, non-bouncing (damping 1) and limited to opacity and transform. On the web, spring curves need CSS `linear()` (Safari 17.2+), and a 2026 WebKit report says such curves can push animations onto the main thread (see Q3, motion).

### Gaps
- Google's full methodology (participant counts per study, statistics, effect sizes, which tasks) was not visible in the excerpts; no peer-reviewed paper or independent replication was found.
- The official m3.material.io motion token values were not retrieved, so the spring parameters above are second-hand.

## 3. Other substantive shifts 2023-2026: bento grids, variable and condensed type, calm technology, data-dense "pro" UIs, depth vs flat, dark mode, visionOS influence, micro-interactions and haptics, AI-native/generative UI

### Takeaway
Most of the period's durable changes are about restraint and hierarchy, not decoration:
- Linear's 2024 and March 2026 redesigns both cut visual noise and dimmed navigation so content leads, with a perceptual (LCH) colour system generated from three inputs.
- Calm technology became a certifiable standard in 2024-25.
- Condensed display type remains the sports convention, but scoreboards pair it with a neutral face for numbers.
- On dark mode, the evidence (BACKGROUND, 2020 and earlier) still favours light mode for reading accuracy in normal vision.
- Web haptics remain a fragile hack in Safari.
- Generative UI is real in consumer search but unproven for repeat-use operational tools.
- Bento grids are mostly a marketing-page pattern with weak evidence.

### Cited Findings

**Bento grids**
- Bento is a modular layout of cells of different sizes. Precursors were Windows Phone tiles, the Windows 8 Start menu, and analytics and finance dashboards (PayPal, Google Ads). Apple popularised it on iPhone landing pages and keynote slides. It builds hierarchy through cell size without extra chrome, and CSS Grid makes it easy to build — [Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/what-is-the-bento-ui-trend-and-how-can-you-get-started); [Studio Meyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/bento-grid-layouts)
- Evidence is weak. One blog cites "35% longer dwell times" without naming a study, and sources call the trend "at peak popularity" — [Studio Meyer](https://studiomeyer.io/en/blog/bento-grid-layouts)

**Data-dense "pro" UIs: Linear, Vercel Geist, Stripe, Raycast**
- Linear, Mar 20 2024: "tabs, headers, filters, and panels are adjusted to reduce the visual noise and clutter". The default themes gained contrast; the Inbox got increased density and better contrast — [Linear changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-03-20-new-linear-ui)
- The theme generator moved from HSL to LCH because LCH keeps perceived lightness consistent across hues. Theme inputs went from 98 variables to three (base colour, accent colour, contrast), and the contrast input auto-generates high-contrast variants for accessibility — [Linear blog, "How we redesigned the Linear UI"](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)
- Linear, Mar 12 2026 refresh (Charlie Aufmann, Maxime Heckel): "not every element deserves equal visual weight". Task-central parts stay in focus, while orientation and navigation recede. Headers, navigation and view controls were made consistent across projects, issues, reviews and documents; icons were redrawn; navigation sidebars were made "slightly dimmer so the main content stands out" — [Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/behind-the-latest-design-refresh); [Linear changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-12-ui-refresh). A secondary summary adds "softened" borders and separators — [Plushcap](https://www.plushcap.com/companies/linear/blog/summaries/2026/03)
- Geist (Journey's body face): Vercel's design system supplies "colors, typography, materials, layout, and React components" — [Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist)
  - The typeface was made with Basement Studio from late 2022 and open-sourced under the SIL OFL, with Sans and Mono cuts — [Basement Studio](https://basement.studio/post/the-birth-of-geist-a-typeface-crafted-for-the-web); [GitHub vercel/geist-font](https://github.com/vercel/geist-font)
  - Release date conflicts: "early 2023" per Basement vs October 2023 per Peerlist (the repo was created Oct 16 2023) — [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/blog/engineering/how-to-use-vercel-geist-font-in-nextjs)
- Stripe's dashboard is ranked first for data density (compact sortable tables, real-time updates, nested information), with money right-aligned for scanning — [AdminLTE roundup (secondary)](https://adminlte.io/blog/25-best-dashboard-design-examples-for-2026-real-products-principles/). The claim that it uses monospaced numerals is unverified (same source)
- Raycast: third-party token extractions disagree. One describes a near-black surface ladder with hairline borders and no shadows; another describes layered shadows with inset highlights — [getdesign.md](https://getdesign.md/design-md/raycast) (third-party; low confidence)

**Variable fonts and condensed display faces in sport**
- Condensed bold sans-serifs dominate sports because they "maximize letter size within constrained jersey and scoreboard spaces". This is BACKGROUND lineage: Compacta in 1970s broadcasting, and ESPN's adoption of Knockout, whose nine widths are each drawn separately — [Font Alternatives](https://fontalternatives.com/best-fonts-for/sports/); [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/compacta-font/)
- Nike's 2024 Olympic typeface (Pizza Typefaces) was a variable font, chosen so one face could shift across training kit and fan apparel. The brief rejected "the usual bold sports fonts" — [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/design/fonts-typography/nikes-olympic-typeface); [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/pizza-typefaces-nike-olympics-graphic-design-290824)
- A critique of the FIFA World Cup 2026 scoreboard notes the custom display face gave way to a neutral sans for the timer and scores so the data would read on TV — [Pimp my Type](https://pimpmytype.com/?p=24064)
- WHOOP's brand guidelines: Proxima Nova for words and DINPro for numbers — [WHOOP Brand & Design Guidelines (PDF)](https://developer.whoop.com/assets/files/WHOOP%20-%20Brand%20%26%20Design%20Guidelines-bdea3554e94b4ea09e68695b1e8dc8e7.pdf) (the same excerpt also mentions "Circular" as a primary face for fallback rules, an internal inconsistency)

**Calm technology / quiet UI**
- Amber Case founded the Calm Tech Institute in May 2024 to create a certification. The first certified products were shown around CES 2025: reMarkable Paper Pro, mui Board Gen 2, Airthings View Plus — [Slashdot via IEEE Spectrum](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/01/22/056208/calm-tech-certification-rewards-less-distracting-tech); [Calm Tech Institute](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-certified)
- The standard scores 81 points across attention, periphery, durability, light, sound and materials. Its rules include "all but the most essential alerts ship switched off" and that a device "should work even when it fails" — [Slashdot/IEEE](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/25/01/22/056208/calm-tech-certification-rewards-less-distracting-tech). Certified companies listed: reMarkable, Mudita, Airthings, mui Lab, Ohm Health — [Calm Tech Institute membership](https://www.calmtech.institute/membership)
- The roots (Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown at Xerox PARC) are BACKGROUND (same sources)

**Depth (neo-skeuomorphic) vs flat; visionOS influence**
- visionOS's main material is an adaptive glass with translucency and specular highlights for depth, with thin, regular and thick variants for buttons, sections and raised layers — [Apple WWDC23 spatial design session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/); legibility guidance in [Create with Swift](https://www.createwithswift.com/ensuring-interface-legibility-and-contrast-in-visionos)
- visionOS hover: interactive elements highlight on gaze so people know what a pinch will trigger. Custom hover effects arrived in visionOS 2, and Apple warns against overusing them because they "distract users and cause visual discomfort" — [Apple WWDC24 session 10152](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10152/)
- A 2025 Apple session recommends rounded shapes for interactive elements because they draw the eye to the centre (excerpt via search; session [WWDC25 303](https://developer-rno.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/303))
- The carry-over to iPadOS is visual (glass, depth, restrained colour), not the input model; no evidence was found of eye-focus hover on iPad — [TechRadar/Federighi](https://www.techradar.com/phones/ios/we-did-all-this-work-with-physical-glass-simulating-as-closely-as-we-could-the-actual-properties-of-glass-for-liquid-glass-says-apples-craig-federighi-then-they-went-further)

**Dark mode (evidence mostly BACKGROUND, pre-2023)**
- NN/g's summary of Düsseldorf studies (2020, BACKGROUND): with normal vision, light mode (positive polarity) "won across all dimensions" for visual-acuity and proofreading tasks in both age groups (18-33 and 60-85), with a smaller advantage for older adults on acuity. There was no fatigue difference. The light-mode advantage grows as text gets smaller, and participants did not perceive the difference. Dark mode may help some low-vision users, such as people with cataracts — [NN/g "Dark Mode vs. Light Mode"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/) (via excerpts and reposts)
- Thai older adults (60-73) on tablets: no task-time difference, but light mode was rated easier to read and preferred — [JMMS study](https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JMMS/article/view/261810)
- Sethi & Ziat (2023, via a thesis): older adults show higher cognitive load with dark mode in bright rooms; younger adults with dark mode in dim rooms — [University of St. Gallen thesis](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/62e4b3c1-0521-4d3a-bbad-e6e5eb81795e/content) (original not accessed)
- WHOOP's dark UI is described as functional (coloured data "pops", less glare at early-morning checks) — [925Studios (third-party)](https://www.925studios.co/blog/whoop-design-breakdown)

**Micro-interactions and haptic feedback (web feasibility)**
- Safari has no Vibration API. Since Safari 18 (one source says 17.4), a non-standard `<input type="checkbox" switch>` fires a system haptic, and libraries trigger it by clicking a hidden label — [WebKit bug 285120](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=285120); [azukiazusa (WebHaptics)](https://azukiazusa.dev/en/blog/ios-safari-web-haptics); [Ionic issue 29942](https://github.com/ionic-team/ionic-framework/issues/29942)
- Limits: iOS only, a fixed system pattern, and liable to break if Apple changes control internals — [ios-haptics on npm](https://npmjs.com/package/ios-haptics)
- One unverified source says Apple patched the JS-triggered path in iOS 26.5 — [iOS Haptics Tester](https://rapidtoolset.com/en/tool/ios-haptics-tester)
- Motion primitives in Safari:
  - CSS `linear()` easing (which can approximate springs): iOS Safari 17.2+ — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-linear-easing); [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-linear-easing-function)
  - Same-document View Transitions: Safari 18. Cross-document: Safari 18.2 — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions); [DevToolbox guide](https://devtoolbox.dedyn.io/blog/css-view-transitions-complete-guide)
  - There is no native CSS `spring()`; Apple proposed it in 2016 and it is still open — [CSSWG issue 280](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/280)
  - WebKit reports: `linear()`/WAAPI animations capped at 30 or 60fps depending on Low Power Mode (2024) — [WebKit bug 281429](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=281429); spring-style `linear()` curves forcing main-thread animation and dropped frames on iOS Safari 26 (Apr 15 2026, closed as a duplicate of 312015) — [WebKit bug 312407](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=312407)

**AI-native / generative UI (2024-2026)**
- Google Research's "Generative UI" ships as Gemini's "dynamic view" experiment and in AI Mode in Search: the model "designs and codes a fully customized interactive response for each prompt" — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)
- Evaluation: "when ignoring generation speed", raters strongly preferred these interfaces to plain LLM text or markdown. Human-expert-built sites still had the highest preference, with generative UI "close behind". Quality "strongly depends" on the underlying model. Google built a benchmark, PAGEN, from expert-made sites (same source)
- Analytics: Tableau Pulse (generally available in Tableau 2024.1) writes natural-language summaries of metric changes. It says whether a change falls within an expected range and suggests follow-up questions — see Q7.

### Inferences
- **For Journey these trends converge on one thing.** Linear's 2026 rule ("not every element deserves equal visual weight; navigation recedes") and calm-tech's "only essential alerts on" describe what Journey has already chosen: Operations "says it once"; orange only for now and go; crimson reserved. The overhaul can lean further this way: dim the frame relative to content during a session, and soften separators. Treat this as substance.
- **LCH / OKLCH-style perceptual colour** generated from a few inputs (base, accent, contrast) is a strong fit for Journey's many feature palettes copied from `equipment.tokens.css`. Generating them from a few inputs, with a contrast knob for a high-contrast floor variant, would replace hand-held copies. Feasibility: CSS `oklch()` works in current Safari; the report writer should confirm the minimum iPadOS on studio iPads.
- **Condensed display plus a neutral numeric face** matches Journey's Saira Condensed plus Geist split. The sports and WHOOP precedent suggests set data (weight, reps, TUL) should be in the neutral face with tabular figures, never the condensed display face.
- **Dark mode.** Gym floors are usually brightly lit. The evidence favours light mode for reading accuracy, especially for small text and for older eyes in bright rooms, so light should stay the default on the floor and dark stay a choice.
- **Haptics on the web are not dependable**, so "tap confirmed" feedback should stay visual (a press state, a brief tick) and never depend on vibration.
- **Generative UI** is not a fit for the trainer's floor (it is non-deterministic, slow and unfamiliar on each view, against "the same spot every glance"). At most, a leader's "ask a question" box could be a candidate, with human-built default views kept.
- **Bento** is useful on the leader's Today/Week pages only as "size equals importance", not as decoration.

### Gaps
- No controlled 2023-2026 evidence was found on bento layouts, neo-skeuomorphic depth vs flat, or micro-interactions affecting task performance.
- No 2023-2026 replication of the dark-vs-light findings on tablets in bright gyms was found.
- Official Raycast and Stripe design-system documentation was not found; their details above are low confidence.
- AI-native patterns are covered more deeply in the sibling note `ai_ui_capabilities.md`.

## 4. Fitness and coaching apps: colour, type, rings and charts, dark mode, and what in-workout screens show and hide

### Takeaway
The 2024-2026 redesigns (WHOOP Home 2025, Oura Oct 2025, Tonal, Strava's Record screen 2025, watchOS 26-27 Workout) share one pattern: one headline state up front, detail one tap away, and the in-workout view reduced to a few user-chosen metrics in big type. Live Activities carry "what's next, sets done, rest countdown" outside the app. Several apps let users hide scores (WHOOP), and coach-led apps (Ladder) put the demo first and push swap, journal and settings behind a swipe. Many apps show prescribed targets and AI coaching cues (Hevy's target weight and reps, Peloton IQ's suggested weights, Workout Buddy). That is precisely what Journey's method rules out, so those parts do not transfer.

### Inferences
- **What transfers to Journey's Active Session:**
  - (a) One glance answers "which machine, which settings, what happened last time", with everything else behind a tap (WHOOP and Oura layering; Ladder pushing secondary actions behind a swipe).
  - (b) Big numerals in a neutral numeric face (WHOOP's DIN for numbers).
  - (c) A trainer-chosen, short metric set (watchOS "Metrics and Views").
  - (d) Showing "what's next" (Hevy's next exercise) as the next machine in the route.
  - (e) Stats and context together without switching screens (Strava 2025).
  - (f) Hiding numbers when the client is watching. WHOOP's "capture but don't see" is a direct analogue for Journey's client-facing Pulse mode.
- **What must NOT transfer:** prescribed target weight and reps (Hevy), suggested weights and form correction (Peloton IQ), and spoken AI coaching (Workout Buddy). Journey's floor rule is "a guide for set-up and a log for outcomes, never a coach, never suggests a progression". Last time's load can be shown as a fact, but never framed as a target.
- **Colour.** WHOOP's red/yellow/green score is a verdict colour. Journey reserves crimson for Critical and rep quality, and "sentences, not scores". A traffic-light grade on a client would contradict both.
- **Coach-side lesson.** Trainerize users complain of a fixed, cluttered multi-client dashboard, and TrueCoach's answer is "who's scheduled today". Journey's Hub and Operations → Today already lead with the day's people.

- No primary design write-ups were found for Future, FitBod, Strong (beyond a competitor's comparison), Apple Fitness+ on iPad, or the iOS 26 iPhone Fitness app. Their in-workout visual specs (type sizes, colours) are unverified.

- No published study measures glance time or error rate in any of these in-workout screens.

## 5. Glanceable and operational UIs beyond fitness: complications, automotive HMI, kitchen display systems, clinical and ops dashboards — which rules carry over to a trainer glancing twice per machine

### Takeaway
The most rigorous glance rules come from automotive:
- NHTSA (2013, BACKGROUND): each glance at most 2 s, at most 12 s total per task (about 6 touches), and text entry locked out while moving.
- Android for Cars: 24dp minimum glanceable text, 32dp for decision-critical text, 4.5:1 contrast, 76x76dp touch targets, task flows of at most 5 steps.

Kitchen display systems add ticket ageing colours (grey → yellow → red, green when done). Apple Watch complications add "timely, up to date, useful at a glance", with tap-through to the full app. Clinical alarm research (BACKGROUND) shows the cost of over-alerting: 64-99% of alarms false or non-actionable, and slower responses as false alarms rise.

### Cited Findings
- **NHTSA Phase 1 guidelines (April 2013, voluntary, BACKGROUND).** Single glances away from the road of no more than 2 seconds and a total of no more than 12 seconds per task. Roughly six touches within 12 s. Recommends disabling manual text entry and video while moving — [US DOT](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/us-dot-releases-guidelines-minimize-vehicle-distractions); [KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/nhtsa-issues-new-distracted-driving-guidelines); [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/2013/04/25/nhtsa-voluntary-guidelines-distracted-driving/); [NHTSA PDF](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/strickland-distraction_guidelines_03122012.pdf)
- **Android for Cars (current guidelines).**
  - Primary text "required for decision making" at 32dp, secondary at 24dp; "Sizes below 24dp are not easily glanceable"
  - Text items at most 120 characters
  - Contrast at least 4.5:1 for icons, text and images that convey information
  - Minimum touch target 76x76dp with at least 23dp between targets
  - Task flows at most 5 steps (a MUST for templated apps); actions not allowed while driving are deferred to the phone
  - Sources: [Android for Cars visual principles](https://developers.google.com/cars/design/design-foundations/visual-principles); [Automotive OS typography](https://developers.google.com/cars/design/automotive-os/design-system/typography); [Android for Cars UX requirements](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/cars/guides/ux-requirements/overview)
- **Kitchen display systems (Toast, GoTab).**
  - Toast supports per-item and per-modifier KDS colours (sizes, allergies, special requests) and coloured ticket borders by dining option — [Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/article/Customize-the-Appearance-of-KDS-Tickets)
  - Toast also offers "Warning Colors" that change as tickets age, and fulfilment colours (yellow at the second expo once the first fulfils, green when everything is fulfilled) — [Toast two-expo workflow](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformKDSWorkflowUsingTwoExpo.html)
  - An Average Fulfillment Timer appears in a screen corner over a recent window — [Toast docs](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/platformguide/platformKitchenAvgFullfillmentTimer.html)
  - GoTab: grey for new, yellow as an order nears its allotted time, red when overdue, green when done — [GoTab docs](https://docs.gotab.io/operator/kds-printers-additional-display-setup/kds-ticket-timer/)
  - A Toast operator asked for whole-ticket recolouring on modified items, which isn't built — [Toast community](https://community.toasttab.com/t5/restaurant-operations/is-there-a-plan-for-this-kds-improvement-or-something-similar/m-p/1571)
- **Apple Watch complications and Smart Stack.** Complications show "timely, relevant information when they lift their wrist" and should be "timely, up to date, and useful". A tap opens the app. The Smart Stack holds up to three complications and surfaces widgets by the wearer's context. WidgetKit updates from a supplied timeline in an energy-efficient way — [Apple WidgetKit docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit); [Apple watchOS apps docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps)
- **Live Activities** (iOS, used by workout apps): real-time metrics plus a minimal control (Hevy's mark-set-done, skip rest) — [Hevy help center](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35649846517399)
- **Clinical alarm overload (BACKGROUND, 2011-2015).**
  - UCSF logged more than 2.5 million alarms in one month across 461 ICU patients; about 88% of arrhythmia alarms were false — [FierceHealthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/it/ucsf-researchers-log-2-5-million-patient-alarms-one-month)
  - CHOP's video study: 86.7% false or non-actionable alarms in ICU and 99% on wards. "Caregiver response times increased as the number of false, or nonactionable, alarms increased" — [CHOP Research](https://research.chop.edu/cornerstone-blog/researchers-honored-using-video-better-understand-alarm-fatigue)
  - AAMI (2011): more than 350 alarms per patient per day in some settings, under 5% requiring intervention. ECRI has ranked alarm hazards #1 or #2 on its Top 10 Technology Hazards since the list began — [PSQH](https://psqh.com/analysis/alarm-fatigue-hazards-the-sirens-are-calling)

### Inferences
- **Translate NHTSA into Journey's own budget.** Each of the two glances per machine should be answerable in under about 2 s: machine name, the client's settings, last time's outcome. The whole per-machine interaction (glance, log, move on) should stay within a handful of touches. The 2 s and 12 s figures are driving-safety limits, not gym limits, but they are the best-evidenced numbers for "eyes away from the primary task". The trainer's primary task is the client.
- **Size budget.** Android for Cars' 24dp floor and 32dp for decision text, and 76dp targets for use while attention is elsewhere, are much larger than Journey's current 40px control floor and 14px body text. That suggests the in-session decision text (settings, last load) and the one or two primary controls should be markedly bigger than elsewhere in the app. The report writer should not present 76dp as a requirement, since a car is not a gym; it is a reference point for "used while attention is elsewhere, at arm's length".
- **The car rule "lock out text entry while moving"** maps to Journey's floor rule: typing a note mid-set should be deferrable to the Wrap-up (which Journey already partly does with the Note for the next trainer).
- **KDS ageing colours fit Journey's timing states,** for example a session running over 20 minutes, or a booking start nobody logged. They must not grade a client's performance (no progression verdicts) and must keep crimson for Critical.
- **Alarm-fatigue evidence supports Journey's "Operations says it once" and "All clear" rules.** Every non-actionable flag slows responses to the actionable ones, so a leader dashboard should count and suppress rather than repeat.

### Gaps
- No airline operations-centre or air-traffic dashboard design sources were found within the period.
- No source specific to gym-floor glance behaviour (glance duration at a machine, one-handed tablet holding) was found.
- The Android for Cars per-glance time limits (beyond the 5-step task cap) were not visible in the excerpts.
- No post-2023 update to the NHTSA guidelines was confirmed.

## 6. iPad-specific design 2024-2026: iPadOS 26 windowing and menu bar, HIG layout guidance, tab bar vs sidebar, Stage Manager, Pencil — what matters for a Home Screen web app in portrait and landscape

### Takeaway
iPadOS 18 (2024) made the tab bar a compact text bar at the top that can expand into a sidebar. Apple recommends 2-5 top-level destinations and the same tabs on iPhone and iPad. iPadOS 26 (2025) added true windowing and a hidden-by-default menu bar. Most relevant to Journey: since iOS/iPadOS 26, Add to Home Screen opens every site as a web app by default, which makes Journey's install path stable and simpler. A web app cannot use the native tab bar, sidebar or menu bar, so it must draw its own. Windowing means Journey can be shown at arbitrary widths, so layouts must respond to window width, not device orientation.

### Cited Findings
- **iPadOS 18 tab bar and sidebar (WWDC24 "Elevate your tab and sidebar experience in iPadOS").** The text-only tab bar floats at the top, in the navigation bar's title area, and "reduces the amount of unused vertical and horizontal space". An optional sidebar button transforms it into a left sidebar, and closing the sidebar slides it back. "Top-level sections belong in the tab bar", and the sidebar can show deeper sections. Apple recommends consistent tabs between iPhone and iPad and warns that too many choices make information harder to find. Users can pin sidebar items to the tab bar — [Apple WWDC24 session 10147](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10147/); [heise](https://heise.de/-9839353)
- Criticism: developer Nico Reese said the change "brings many complex issues" and felt like going backward; Federico Viticci raised concerns about Books — [Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2024/06/19/tab-bar-and-sidebar-in-ipados-18)
- Secondary HIG summary (a third-party skill file citing the HIG component pages, not Apple's text): 2-5 destinations, single-word labels, don't hide or disable tabs when their content is empty — [skills.sh ios-components (third-party)](https://www.skills.sh/jpoindexter/design-and-ai-skills/ios-components)
- **iPadOS 26 menu bar.**
  - New, Mac-like, and "tuned for touch and pointer". Hidden by default; revealed by swiping down from the top of a window or with Globe+M. It holds app commands, shortcuts and window management. MacStories wishes it were always visible — [MacStories iOS and iPadOS 26 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/11)
  - It can be switched off under Settings > Multitasking & Gestures — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1981425/ipados-26-settings-to-change/)
  - Critics found it "mostly useless" in apps without real menus — [Ark's Newsletter (tested on iPad mini 7)](https://ark.beehiiv.com/p/i-tested-ipados-26-on-the-ipad-mini-7-surprising-things-apple-didnt-tell-you)
- **iPadOS 26 windowing and Liquid Glass.** The windowing system received "the heaviest application of the look" — [AppleInsider iPadOS 26](https://appleinsider.com/inside/ipados-26)
- **Home Screen web apps in iOS/iPadOS 26.** "Every website added to the Home Screen opens as a dedicated web app, regardless of developer configuration". An "Open as Web App" toggle in the share sheet is on by default; turning it off makes a plain bookmark. Previously this required a manifest `display` value or a meta tag. Manifest-based web app support is not removed. Apple's rationale is consistency with web apps in the Mac Dock (Sept 2023) — [heise](https://heise.de/-10749652); [Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=49472); [Gigazine on Safari 26](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250623-safari-26-webkit-beta)
- **Touch targets.** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA, 2023) sets 24x24 CSS px minimum targets, or spacing so that 24px circles don't overlap — [DAISY KB](https://kb.daisy.org/publishing/docs/wcag/target-size-minimum.html). Apple's HIG asks for at least 44x44 pt hit regions (60x60 pt on visionOS), quoted in Apple's developer forums — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/687200). WCAG 2.5.5 (AAA) asks for 44x44 CSS px (same DAISY source)
- **Older-device performance.** See Q1 for A14 vs A17 Pro. A carrier troubleshooting page for the iPad 10th generation on iPadOS 26 addresses "my tablet is running slowly" with only a restart — [Vodafone device guide](https://deviceguides.vodafone.co.uk/apple/ipad-10th-generation-ipados-26/troubleshooting/basic-use/my-tablet-is-running-slowly/solved/false)

### Inferences
- **Journey's iPad navigation.** The iPadOS 18 model (top-level places in a compact bar that can expand into a sidebar in landscape) is a well-trodden pattern for Operations' seven destinations and the Admins dashboard's four places. Apple's 2-5 guidance suggests Operations' seven are at the upper bound of what a bar should hold. That supports a sidebar in landscape and grouping in portrait, which the Admins dashboard already does. All of this has to be drawn in HTML/CSS; nothing native is available to a web app.
- **Windowing.** With iPadOS 26 windowing and Stage Manager, a Home Screen web app can be resized to arbitrary widths. Journey's layouts should key off container or viewport width (CSS container queries) rather than portrait/landscape. The existing `usePhone()` "under 600px wide" breakpoint will trigger inside a narrow window on an iPad, which may be desirable but should be deliberate.
- **The default web-app change simplifies onboarding a new studio iPad.** Add to Home Screen will always give a standalone app. It also means a mistaken "Open as Web App: off" yields a Safari bookmark with browser chrome. A setup checklist item is worth having.
- **Size floors.** Journey's 40px floor sits between WCAG's 24px AA and Apple's 44pt. For the one-handed, arm's-length session screen, 44px or more (and more for primary actions, see Q5) is the defensible floor.
- **The menu bar and Pencil** do not reach a web app in any documented way. Pencil works as a pointer and for handwriting into text fields (Scribble), but this was not researched.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed whether a Home Screen web app gets any iPadOS 26 menu-bar commands, or how it behaves in Stage Manager or windowing (minimum window size, safe areas). This needs testing on a device.
- Apple Pencil and Scribble behaviour in web inputs on iPadOS 26 was not researched.
- The current HIG "Layout" page guidance for iPad (size classes, margins) was not reachable.
- Sibling note `web_platform_ipad_safari.md` covers Safari specifics in more depth.

## 7. Dashboards for managers 2023-2026: narrative and "sentences not scores" summaries, AI summaries, anomaly-first layouts, sparklines and small multiples, and evidence on overload

### Takeaway
The 2023-2026 shift in management dashboards is from a wall of charts to a headline plus drill-down:
- Tableau Pulse (generally available in 2024.1) writes natural-language summaries of metric changes, says whether a change is within the expected range, and proposes follow-up questions.
- Consumer health apps moved the same way (Oura's news-app "One Big Thing" Today; WHOOP's top three dials with deep dives).
- Linear's 2026 refresh made the same move at the chrome level ("not every element deserves equal visual weight").

The evidence base is older and indirect: NN/g's operational vs analytical distinction and pre-attentive attributes (BACKGROUND), and clinical alarm fatigue showing that non-actionable signals slow responses. No independent study was found measuring AI-summary accuracy or its effect on managers' decisions.

### Cited Findings
- **NN/g on dashboards (undated in the excerpt; treated as BACKGROUND).** "Dashboards are collections of data visualizations, presented in a single-page view that imparts at-a-glance information on which users can act quickly. Their goal is not to facilitate exploration; instead, they provide information that can be consumed fast, with a minimum of interaction or cognitive processing." It distinguishes operational dashboards ("impart critical information quickly to users as they are engaged in time-sensitive tasks") from analytical ones (less time pressure) — [NN/g "Dashboards: Making Charts and Graphs Easier to Understand"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)
  - Secondary recaps: length and position are decoded fastest, and bold colour should be saved for the single key number because overused emphasis cancels itself out — [WellSky engineering](https://engineering.wellsky.com/post/how-to-use-pre-attentive-attributes-to-communicate-data-visually); [Qlik design blog (BACKGROUND, 2014)](https://community.qlik.com/blogs/qlikviewdesignblog/2014/01/17/pre-attentive-processing-for-dashboard-design)
- **AI and narrative summaries: Tableau Pulse.**
  - Generally available in Tableau 2024.1 and included in all Tableau Cloud editions — [Tableau blog](https://tableau.com/blog/release-tableau-pulse-metrics-layer-viz-navigation); [Tableau 2024.1 features](https://www.tableau.com/en-gb/2024-1-features)
  - It uses generative AI to write insight summaries in natural language, flag unusual movement, say whether a change is within a standard or expected range, detect drivers, trends, contributors and outliers, and suggest follow-up questions. It delivers by email, Slack and mobile, with a Metrics Layer as the "single source of truth" for metric definitions — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchbusinessanalytics/news/366571065/Tableau-launches-Pulse-a-GenAI-fueled-insight-generator); [Salesforce Tableau Pulse page](https://www.salesforce.com/au/analytics/tableau/pulse/)
  - It initially supported US English only — [Tableau 2024.1 features](https://www.tableau.com/en-gb/2024-1-features)
- **Headline-first consumer dashboards.** Oura's Today is modelled on "a news app's headline page" with "One Big Thing" — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/20/oura-app-redesign/). WHOOP puts three dials on top with deep dives and user-chosen metrics — [WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/the-all-new-whoop-home-screen/). Tonal's homepage carries progress trends that tap into history — [Tonal](https://www.tonal.com/blog/the-tonal-mobile-app-redesigned/)
- **Anomaly-first and recede-the-chrome.** Linear 2026: task-central elements stay in focus, navigation recedes, sidebars are dimmed — [Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)
- **Coach-dashboard complaint.** Trainerize users report a layout "too fixed and cluttered when managing many clients" — [ABC Trainerize ideas board](https://ideas.abcfitness.com/forums/167887-coach-trainer-abc-trainerize/suggestions/51342775-trainerize-dashboard-design-needs-better-customiza)
- **Overload evidence (clinical, BACKGROUND).** Response times lengthen as false and non-actionable alarms increase — [CHOP Research](https://research.chop.edu/cornerstone-blog/researchers-honored-using-video-better-understand-alarm-fatigue). Under 5% of up to 350+ daily alarms per patient needed intervention — [PSQH](https://psqh.com/analysis/alarm-fatigue-hazards-the-sirens-are-calling)
- **Generative UI evaluation (Google, 2025):** human-expert-designed pages are still preferred over model-generated ones, and the study ignored generation latency — [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)

### Inferences
- **The 2023-2026 industry direction validates Journey's existing Operations rules:** a line of counts, the nightly note said once, "All clear", the proof one tap away, "sentences, not scores", each with a named minimum sample. Tableau Pulse's "is this within the expected range?" is the AI-era version of Journey's "named minimum sample", and Journey should keep doing it deterministically (rules it can stand behind), not with a language model guessing.
- **Where an LLM summary could add value for a leader,** it should follow Pulse's governance pattern: one metric definition (Journey's single-rule modules such as `states.ts`, `lanes.ts`, `brief.ts`), with the AI only phrasing a sentence about numbers the rules computed, never computing them. That keeps "a confident wrong number is worse than a missing one".
- **Anomaly-first ordering** (what changed or needs action first, steady state collapsed) plus small per-trainer or per-client rows in name order (recognition, never ranking) is consistent with both NN/g's operational-dashboard definition and Journey's no-ranking rule.
- **Sparklines and small multiples** are a natural fit for Ahead's "Clients" two-clock line and Month's per-trainer rows. Evidence for them is classic (Tufte) rather than 2023-2026, and was not found in this research.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 controlled study of AI-generated dashboard summaries (accuracy, trust, decision quality) was found.
- No current evidence on sparklines and small multiples, or on "dashboard overload" in business settings outside healthcare, was found within the time limit.
- NN/g's 2023-2026 dashboard articles (if any) could not be fetched. The NN/g piece cited above is undated in the excerpt.
- Power BI Copilot, Looker or ThoughtSpot narrative features were not researched.
