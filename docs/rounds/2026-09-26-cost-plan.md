# The cost plan: today's schedule first, everything else when it changes

*Sep 26 2026. A proposal, not code. Almost every piece is inside the Mindbody
integration, a Cloud Function or the Firestore structure, so each phase below
names the OK it needs before anyone writes a line. The arithmetic is
`docs/business/cost-plan-model.py`, which loads the Sep 25 model
(`running-costs-model.py`) unchanged and adds only this plan's knobs, so
"today" and "the plan" are measured the same way.*

---

## The short answer

**At 40 studios the plan takes the bill from about $11–17 a studio a month to
about $6–9.** Leave out Render's fixed $52, which does not grow with studios,
and the part that multiplies by forty goes from **$10–16 a studio to
$4.50–7.50**. It does that without making today's schedule any less right.

**It is built on AJ's ranking (Sep 26):**
- Today's schedule has to be right within about 30 minutes, and tomorrow the same.
- The rest of the month can be refreshed once each morning.
- Packages matter when a sale happens.
- Client details matter when they change.

Everything Journey pulls on a timer that the ranking says it does not need is
either slowed down or replaced by Mindbody telling us.

**Syncing every client before a studio launches is cheap: about $2 a studio,
once, about $60 for all forty.** What costs money is *keeping* everyone synced
by asking Mindbody over and over. So the pre-launch sync is one careful run,
and afterwards clients stay current because Mindbody tells us when something
changed.

**At four studios the savings are small in dollars (about $20–40 a month).**
Render is 70–85% of what is left. The plan matters because the per-studio part
is what gets multiplied by forty, and because Jan 1 is when that starts.

**Three things are not known yet, and each changes the plan:**
1. **Is the Mindbody webhook on?** Nothing in the repo records the
   `webhooks-on` step running, and AJ doesn't know. Three of the four answers
   lean on it. This is step zero.
2. **What Mindbody really charges.** The invoice suggests the account may be
   on a daily free allowance, not the 5,000-a-month tier on the pricing page.
   One question to Mindbody settles it (below). If the allowance is per site
   per day, the Mindbody line is close to **$0** for any studio with its own
   site.
3. **Two Mindbody behaviours nobody has tested.** Do the `clientContract.*` /
   `clientSale.*` webhook events exist? Does `staffappointments` accept
   `AppointmentIds`? A test subscription and a one-call probe settle each.

## Part A — Today's schedule: just as right, for less

The pull that keeps today right is the one thing this plan protects. Everything
in this part either keeps that guarantee or strengthens it.

### A0. Find out whether the webhook is on (no code, 2 minutes)

On the PC, in the project folder, in PowerShell:

```
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\scripts\ship\ship-lean-sync.ps1 webhooks-check
```

It only reads. It lists every subscription Mindbody holds for the webhook,
with its status and events.

- **If there's an active subscription with `appointmentBooking.updated` in
  it**, run `webhooks-test` next and do its three-minute test in Mindbody.
- **If there's none, or it's inactive:** `webhooks-on`, then `webhooks-test`.
  This deploys the webhook Cloud Function, the one step in the lean-sync ship
  that `golive` left out on purpose.

**Why it's step zero:**
- Today's bookings can be right from the pull alone.
- But "client details only when they change" and "packages only when a sale
  happens" mean nothing unless Mindbody is telling us.
- And every day it is off, each same-day cancellation costs a month pull.

### A1. The pull: every 30 minutes (a setting now; a default in code later)

**Now, no code.** Operations → Mindbody → sync interval → **30**, at each
studio. That's `Studio.syncIntervalMinutes`, the field saved at
`src/features/admin/mindbody/AdminMindbodyTab.tsx:513`.

**In code.** `DEFAULT_INTERVAL_MINUTES` 15 → 30
(`src/features/admin/syncPolicy.ts:42`), so a new studio starts there.

- **What trainers see:** a change to today or tomorrow shows within about 30
  minutes at worst, while an iPad at the studio has Journey open. With the
  webhook on, it shows within seconds, and the pull is only the safety net.
- **What it saves:** about 28 calls a studio a day, the plan's biggest single
  cut.

### A2. The month: once each morning

`DEEP_PULL_HOURS` `[0, 10, 14, 18]` → `[0]` (`syncPolicy.ts:199`).

The first pull of the studio's day (about 4:30am with the default shift hours)
still reaches the whole month. The 10am, 2pm and 6pm month pulls stop.

A booking made for three or more days out then shows:
- at once by the webhook,
- the next morning without it,
- or at once on Refresh for anything in the next week.

That is AJ's "once each morning". Saves 6–15 calls a studio a day.

### A3. A booking that leaves today or tomorrow: check that one booking

When the near pull loses a booking, it can't tell a cancellation from a move to
next week. Today it asks for the whole month to find out (`settleSweepWith`,
`useAutoSync.ts:160-178`): 2 pages at Solon, about 8 on the shared site.

Asking Mindbody for **that one appointment by its id** answers the same
question in one call:
- It comes back on a new date → it moved.
- It doesn't come back → it was cancelled.

**Needs a one-call probe first:** does `appointment/staffappointments` honour
`AppointmentIds`? Nobody has tested it. Worth it mainly while the webhook is
off, or on the days it misses one.

### A4. Keep the Mindbody sign-in for its real life

`server/mindbody-client.ts:289` throws the staff token away after 55 minutes
and signs in again: about 17 calls a site a day.

Mindbody documents the token as lasting **days** when unused, and renewable.
Keep it until Mindbody says it has expired (a 401), then sign in once. The
invoice will show whether sign-ins count as calls. If they do, this is ~17 a
day a site for free.

**In the same change**, send the four routes that still call Mindbody with a
bare `fetch` through the rate limiter and the breaker, like everything else.
They are `issueUserToken`, `staff`, `staff-image` and `locations`
(`server.ts:175`, `:236`, `:405`). It isn't a saving; it's the floor under the
pre-launch sync.

### A5. The morning name check: only for clients Journey doesn't know

The day's first month pull looks **every** client up, to catch a name or phone
changed in Mindbody (`syncPolicy.ts` `isFirstDeepOfDay`).

With `client.updated` arriving by webhook, that's AJ's "only when it
changes". So look up only clients Journey has never named.

**Do this after A0 shows the webhook delivering, not before.** Saves 4–11
calls a studio a day.

### A6. The "New" that isn't: fix it before any studio's cutover date is set

`src/lib/prior-history.ts:300` returns **complete** when Mindbody has given no
visit count and the client's first Journey session is on or after the
studio's cutover:

```
/* Mindbody said nothing, but she started after the studio moved over. */
if (cutover && client?.firstJourneyDay) return "complete";
```

That was written when the visit count was believed to arrive on every pull.
The Sep 22 plan found it only arrives with a Master Sync.

**What goes wrong.** Launch day, a twelve-year client nobody has synced yet
has her first Journey session. From then on she reads as a whole story:
- "New" and "#1" on the Hub,
- "First time" on machines,
- "Client since" today.

This is exactly the case the comment above it (`:263-266`) warns about, and
the "a confident wrong number" rule forbids it.

**The fix.** With no Mindbody count, the answer is **unknown**, not complete.
A client who really is new still reads complete, because her Master Sync
brings a visit count of 0–2 (`NEW_CLIENT_MAX_VISITS`). The comment at
`:273-279` ("it arrives on every pull") and its twin in
`src/lib/client-coverage.ts:35-38` get corrected with it.

No Mindbody change, no OK needed. It is the cheapest accuracy fix in this
document, and the pre-launch sync (Part B) is what makes the count present
for everyone who matters on day one.

### A7. Know the webhook is still on

Mindbody switches a subscription off after repeated failed deliveries, and
doesn't guarantee order or single delivery.

Relying on it for client details and packages needs two cheap things:

- **A daily look at the subscription's status** (Mindbody's webhooks API
  reports it, with delivery counts). Nothing about this goes out to anyone
  (nothing contacts clients or trainers). It is the line Operations → Mindbody
  shows, and the Overview can raise it as something to look at.
- **A count of what the pull caught that the webhook missed.** Each near pull
  already knows which bookings changed. It can note the ones whose Journey row
  had not been touched by a webhook event since the last pull. A few weeks of
  that number near zero is the proof that the webhook can be trusted.
  It can also be the trigger that puts A5 into effect.

### What is deliberately NOT proposed

- **A 60-minute pull when the webhook is healthy.** It would halve what's left
  of the pull. But if the webhook silently drops one event while reporting
  healthy, a same-day cancellation could take an hour: past AJ's 30. Revisit
  after a month of A7's miss count.
- **Stopping the pull at night.** It already stops an hour after close
  (`PULL_HOURS_MARGIN_MINUTES`, `syncPolicy.ts:254`); nothing more to cut.
- **The shared site's location filter** (`scripts/probe-mindbody-location-filter.ts`,
  never run). With the month pulled once a day it saves ~5 calls a day for the
  three shared studios. Still worth one run, because if it works the
  pre-launch "who counts" pull for those studios is a third the size.

## Part B — The pre-launch sync: every client who matters, a few days before launch

### What it costs, and why that isn't the constraint

| One studio | Finding who counts | Syncing them (5 calls each) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 150 active clients | ~21 calls (~61 on the shared site) | 750 | **~$1.55** |
| 210 | ~29 (~85) | 1,050 | **~$2.16** |
| 300 | ~41 (~121) | 1,500 | **~$3.09** |
| **All 40 studios at ~150** | | | **~$62, once** |

Batching the calls (20 clients to a lookup, many to a packages call) would cut
that to about $1 a studio. **It isn't worth it.** It would mean rewriting the
live sync path to save $40 across the whole rollout. The Master Sync the
profile button already runs is covered by 32 tests; using it unchanged is worth
more than the dollar.

**Money is not the constraint. Correctness, pace and proof are.** The few days
before a launch are for looking at the results between steps, not for staying
under a budget.

### Who counts (AJ's answer)

**Anyone booked in the next 30 days, plus anyone who trained in the last 6
months.**

Found with **one appointments pull** over the studio's
`[today − 6 months, today + 30 days]`: pages of 500, 20–120 calls. The client
ids in it are the list.

- A booking cancelled early doesn't come back from Mindbody, which is right:
  they didn't train.
- On the shared site, run the three studios together, so the site is read once.

Everyone else (the long-gone, the leads) is **not** synced in advance. They
are synced the first time they're booked (B5).

### B1. Extract Master Sync's pure half (the Sep 22 plan's first step, still not done)

`buildMasterSyncPatch` decides every field written to a client:
- only what changed,
- never blanking a field because Mindbody sent a blank.

But `src/lib/mindbody-master-sync.ts` imports the browser Firebase SDK, so a
script on firebase-admin can't use it.

Move the pure half into its own module, with the timestamp injected, the way
`mindbody-commercial-map.ts` already is for the renewals job. The 32 existing
tests import it by name, so if they pass, the move is faithful.

### B2. The onboarding script: `scripts/onboard-studio.ts`

It copies the house safety model (`scripts/backfill-client-since.ts`), nearly
word for word:

- **Dry run by default**; nothing written without `--commit`.
  Flags: `--studio`, `--limit`, `--key`.
- **A client already Master-Synced is skipped, always**, so a re-run costs
  nothing.
- **Only named fields are written** (`update()`), so a bug can't touch the
  rest of the record.
- **A resume log**, appended only after a client fully succeeds, so a stopped
  run restarts where it stopped. The pattern is
  `scripts/migrate-canonical-client-ids.ts:360-375`.
- **A JSON report of every decision** to `backups/`: who counted and why,
  synced, failed (with Mindbody's words), not found.
- **The client-identity rule.** It uses `src/lib/mindbody-site.ts` to decide
  `clients/{id}` vs `clients/{site}-{id}`, exactly as the pull and the webhook
  do. The collision check already found **43** shared numbers between the two
  sites.
- **Its own gentle pace.** About 2 calls a second, run in the evening after
  the studios' pull hours. The rate limiter is per process, so a script on the
  PC doesn't share Render's. 1,500 calls at 2 a second is about 13 minutes.

### B3. Update the report to the real price

`scripts/mindbody-sync-report.ts:45` still prices with "1,000 free a day".
Point it at whatever Mindbody's answer turns out to be, and give it the same
"who counts" rule, so its `notSynced` list is **the scope**, not every client
document.

### B4. The runbook for one launch (T = launch day)

| When | Step | Who | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before T−5 | A0 done and the webhook tested; A6 shipped; B1–B3 merged | | Changes made during the run aren't lost, and nobody reads as "New" |
| T−5 | `npx tsx scripts/onboard-studio.ts --studio <id>` (dry run) | AJ | The list: how many, who, what it will cost. Anyone missing? Anyone who shouldn't be there? |
| T−4 | `… --commit --limit 25` | AJ | Open 5 of them next to Mindbody: name, phone, birthday, visit count, package and sessions left |
| T−3 | `… --commit` (the rest; safe to stop and re-run) | AJ | The report's failed list is empty, or explained |
| T−2 | `npx tsx scripts/mindbody-sync-report.ts` | AJ | **`notSynced` for the scope is empty.** `notLinked` goes to a person to fix in Mindbody |
| T−1 | Set the studio's cutover date (My Studio → Studio) | studio leader | The Hub's numbers and "New" now read from real counts |
| T | Launch. B5 takes over | | |

**Why the proof is the other way round** (Sep 22 plan): the run's own report
only knows who it reached. The proof is a list of everyone in scope who has
**not** been synced, and it has to be empty.

### B5. After launch: everyone else, the first time they're booked

**The rule.** Anyone on today's or tomorrow's schedule who has **never** been
Master-Synced gets synced automatically:
- new clients,
- the lapsed client who comes back,
- the lead who books a first session.

It is the Sep 22 plan's "Piece 1", narrowed by AJ's answers: never-synced
only. There's no staleness re-sync, because details now arrive by webhook,
and a visit count only decides once whether Journey holds the whole story.

**Where it runs:** the nightly renewals job, which already reads the next 122
days of bookings company-wide (`server/renewals-job.ts`). It syncs tomorrow's
never-synced clients before the studio opens, within a budget, and reports
what it skipped. A same-day booking by someone never synced still gets their
name from the pull. Their visit count is unknown until the next night, which
after A6 reads as "unknown", never "New".

**Cost:** 10–15 new clients a studio a month × 5 calls: about **$0.15 a
studio a month**.

## Part C — Packages and client details: only when they change

### Client details: nothing to build

`client.updated` is subscribed (`scripts/mindbody/register-webhook.js:79-90`)
and handled. It even carries the visit count, which the webhook writes
(`functions/src/mindbody/index.ts:670-674`). The profile's Sync button stays.

Once A0 shows the webhook delivering, A5 turns off the daily look-up of
everyone.

### Packages: on a sale, with one exception for AJ to decide

**What Mindbody sends.** Membership events are subscribed and written.
- `clientContract.*` is on the "maybe" list (`register-webhook.js:91`), because
  nobody has confirmed Mindbody sends it.
- `clientSale.*` isn't subscribed, and has no handler.
- **No event carries the sessions remaining**, so every event still needs a
  pull of that client's pricing options.

**The design:**

1. **A sale marks the client.** A contract, membership or sale event puts a
   "packages changed" time on the client. The nightly job pulls every marked
   client first: 2 calls each.
   Needs:
   - a test subscription to learn which event names Mindbody really sends,
   - a small webhook change to write the mark,
   - a new rank in `src/features/renewals/job-plan.ts`.
2. **A monthly sweep of active clients only.** It catches a sale made some way
   no event reports. Past clients, with no visit or booking in the window,
   are never swept. A new booking (B5) or an event wakes them.
   Today the job sweeps every client document with a Mindbody id, past ones
   included.
3. **The exception: near the end of a package, pull the morning of their
   session.** Mindbody's "remaining" goes down with every visit. So a pull
   only on a sale leaves Journey reading "24 left" for a client with 3 left,
   and the renewal conversation is the one moment that number is used.
   Journey already knows how many sessions it logged since the last pull.
   When that count says a client is within a few sessions of the end, the
   nightly job pulls them fresh the morning of their next booking.
   - The number on screen is always Mindbody's own. The count only decides
     **when** to ask.
   - It is 1–3 clients a studio a day.

**AJ: is 3 in, or strictly on a sale?** Without it, the renewals list has to
say how old its number is ("as of Sep 3"), because a confident wrong count is
the thing this app refuses to show.

**The cap stops binding.** The nightly cap of 300 clients (`DEFAULT_MAX_PULLS`,
`server/renewals-job.ts:64`; `RENEWALS_MAX_PULLS`, `render.yaml:266`) is
reached from about 25 studios on today's rules. On these rules demand is about
half, and the cap stays a safety rail rather than a queue.

  uses the Render dashboard, check whether the free plan covers what Journey
  uses (a web service and two cron jobs). If it does, that's another $25.
