## The round in one place

### The brief

AJ, Sep 24: build the packages screen, with creative liberty. **Packages are not decided in the consultation**, so a trainer whose client has no package should see the package information on the **post-session screen**, which has the least to say about a new client. The screen explains the three commitments to a person deciding, in a way that makes the price easy to hear.

### AJ's decisions (Sep 24, the proposal's comments and this chat)

1. **After the last payment depends on the studio.** "Auto renew isn't on everywhere but some studios do have it", and **sessions never expire**, anywhere. Studios control most aspects of their table.
2. **The 30-day money-back covers monthly payers.**
3. **The trainer recommends one length, and it starts on 12 months.** The trainer's own ("Sam's recommendation"), never "most popular".
4. **Money fallbacks are trainer notes**, in this order: the guarantee (the big one: money back, or six months at another gym), the lowest rate on the shortest commitment, once a week, a few more sessions (extra free workouts, or bonus sessions on the package).
5. **Upgrading within the first 30 days, or at renewal, is still true**, however they pay; the contract change is done in Mindbody.
6. "You only lose a session if you cancel within 24 hours" (the proposal).

### What the trainer gets

- **On the post-session screen**, for a client with no package on file (Mindbody shows none, Journey can't tell yet, or the package ended): a card, "Packages at Westlake", with one sentence about the client's package and **Walk through the packages**. For a prospect (Journey holds their whole story, or a temporary profile) the card also lists the lengths with their prices. A client with a live package sees the Renewal conversation button instead, as before; a client who is away sees neither.
- **The packages sheet**, full screen, turned toward the client:
  - the one line about the training, then the lengths side by side (months, name, sessions, and the price as a session, a week or each payment);
  - the chosen length in full: the big price, the bill, the whole package, and the sessions drawn as dots, one small grid per payment;
  - every 4 weeks as steps, and "A longer commitment lowers every payment" only when this studio's table shows it;
  - the Double Transformation Guarantee, with the 30-day upgrade line;
  - Your week (the days they're booked lit, and two more to pick);
  - Life happens: a weeks-away stepper and a timeline;
  - after your last payment.
  - Paying in full is shown as one payment, never as a bill every 4 weeks.
  - One column on every portrait iPad, two in landscape.
- **The trainer notes**, a separate view behind the head's button, never on screen with the client's: the recommendation (move it or clear it), the money fallbacks in AJ's order with the studio's own numbers ($54 a session on The Trial, 6 payments of $432, on the standard table), a once-a-week package the trainer may put on the client's screen, the Academy's own lines, whose prices these are, and anything the table could not show honestly.
- **My Studio → Studio → Renewals** asks, per package, "When the payments finish": not said (the screen stays quiet), it renews automatically, or it doesn't renew by itself.

### The data decisions

- **No index or Cloud Functions change, and no rules change for the packages screen** (the one rules change in this release is the cross-train fix below). The one new stored field, `renewsAutomatically`, sits inside a package row of `studios/{s}/config/renewals`, which the rules don't inspect, and is written only once a studio answers (AJ: "let studios have control over a lot of these aspects"). Only the packages screen reads it; the renewal engine still reads Mindbody's own flag on each contract.
- **Prices are the studio's own table**, read once per screen. Nothing with a dollar sign is drawn until the table has loaded for THIS studio: a refused read never shows the defaults, and a studio switch never shows the last studio's table for a render (`prices-state.ts`; `useRenewalSettings` now says which studio its table is for and whether it holds a package table of its own).
- **Whose table:** the client's home studio (the nightly job prices them against it), falling back to where the session was hosted.
- **One rule for what the whole package comes to** (`renewals/options.ts` `wholePackage`), shared with the Renewal Brief: the billed amount when it agrees with sessions × rate; a table that doesn't add up shows no total at all on the packages screen.
- **Who is offered the packages** is one pure answer (`package-standing.ts`) read live from the client's Mindbody records with the engine's own `pickContracts` and `sessionBalance`. Only "none" says there is no package, and it says when Mindbody was checked. Prices sit on the post-session screen itself only for a client whose whole story Journey holds; a long-standing client is never shown a price list because a record looks empty.
- **The recommendation is held on screen only.** It will live on the consultation record (build step 2, which needs AJ's Firestore OK).

### What the round found and fixed on the way

- **Two small honesty fixes on the post-session screen** (`11896b4`): a machine name in Today wraps instead of being cut off, and the dose Dial says "Saved" only when the write succeeded.
- **The Renewal Brief and the packages screen share one money formatter** (`renewals/money.ts`), which rounds before it decides whether to show cents.

### A cross-train visitor finishes her session (the handoff's §3)

AJ asked first for a plain answer: can a trainer cross-training at another studio actually finish a session? **No.** Proved on the emulator: approving a client to cross-train opens her record to READ at the other studio, but the rules let only her home studio's trainers write it, and Finish wrote her totals (session count, last session, the machine weights, the tallies) in the same all-or-nothing batch as the session and its sets. The refused totals took the whole session with them, so the visitor tapped Finish and nothing saved. AJ: "fix it and ship it".

- **The rules** (`firestore.rules`, `crossTrainSessionTotals`): a trainer whose studio the client is approved at (home, accessible or guest studio) may update her document when every field the write changes is one of the sixteen Finish, Start and a History delete write. Her name, her home, her approvals, her medical history and `renewal` stay refused, and a trainer at a studio she is not approved at is refused as before.
- **Finish** (`src/lib/sync-utils.ts` `completeWorkoutSession`): the batch now holds the session, the sets and the settings; the client's totals are written after it, on their own. A refusal of the totals costs the totals, never the session, and the Active Session says so: "Session saved. {name}'s session count and starting weights didn't update from this iPad."
- **Tested:** seven rules tests (`a cross-train visitor's session`: the full eight-machine Finish, split and as one batch, which fits the rules' expression budget; Start's stamps; a History delete's give-backs; the refusals; a third studio refused; the home trainer unchanged) and `sync-utils.finish.test.ts` (the shape of what Finish writes, and a refused total that keeps the session). The trap is `docs/KNOWN-TRAPS.md#cross-train`; the iPad walk is **Round 18**.

### How it ships

`scripts/ship/ship-packages.ps1`, `prepare` then `golive`, run from the project folder on `packages-screen` (Claude switches the folder once the landing has shipped). **Ship the landing first**: this branch contains it, so shipping this one first would carry the landing too (both ways are safe, and `prepare` says which). **The rules deploy first**: `prepare` runs the rules tests and stops if they fail; `golive` deploys `firestore.rules` to production before anything else (they only add access, so the running app is unaffected), then makes and pushes the restore tag `restore/2026-09-24-before-packages`, then pushes the branch to `master`. No index or Functions deploy. Then reload every iPad and walk **Rounds 17 and 18** of `docs/ops/TESTING-CHECKLIST.md`.

To undo the app: `master` goes back to the restore tag. The rules can stay: the app before this round writes the totals in one batch as it always did, and the new branch only lets a visitor's batch through. To undo them too, deploy `firestore.rules` from the restore tag. In Firestore, a `renewsAutomatically` a studio saved stays inside its package row, where the app before this round simply ignores it.
