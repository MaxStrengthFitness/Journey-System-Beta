`bb90d773`, then this write-up. The list was the second wave's "Not built"

## Built

- **`281706a` Overdue setup items on Home.** A studio opening (stage
  Setting up or Handed over) whose checklist has an item past its due day is
  an item of What needs you: `id: "launch-overdue"`, kind Launches, tone
  watch. One studio: "Mentor's setup item “Each unit's name and starting
  settings checked” was due Mon, Sep 21, 2026." (or "Mentor has 3 setup
  items overdue; the oldest, …"), its proof "Aragorn leads Mentor. Tick it,
  skip it with a reason, or move the opening day." (or "Nobody leads Mentor
  yet."), its door the studio's Setup, where the checklist is. Several
  studios: "2 studios opening have setup items overdue: Rivendell and
  Mentor.", the oldest named, the door Launches. Its condition is every
  overdue item's id, so Take it, Snooze and Dismiss hold until an item is
  done, skipped or falls due, and the mark ends once the read has answered.
  `home/overdue-setup.ts` is the pure half (`launchingIds`, `overdueSetup`),
  over the checklist's own `buildChecklist` and its own word "overdue" (an
  item still to do, or whose fact couldn't be read, after its due day).
  **The reads:** the dashboard makes the two small reads Launches makes
  (`launches/useSetupData.ts`: a studio's `setupItems` and its `roster`
  count) for each studio opening, once when it opens and again on Check
  again — usually no studio is opening, and then nothing is read. No new
  index (subcollection gets). A checklist that couldn't be read is named in
  Home's Couldn't check ("Couldn't check Rivendell's setup checklist."),
  never counted as on time. "The owner": a setup item names none (AJ's q1),
  so the item names who leads the studio (`leadersOf`: its studio owner,
  studio leader or head trainer, in name order).
- **`7c83c65` A studio's Activity on My Studio.** My Studio → Studio ends
  with **Activity**: what head office changed at this studio from the Admins
  dashboard, the newest fifty entries, newest first, the same quiet rows as
  Admins (`ActivityList`), read-only, with Try again on a failed read.
  `admins/activity/useStudioActivityLive.ts` is the read: ONE bounded
  listener (`activity where studioId == s, orderBy at desc, limit 50`, the
  index the second wave shipped), let go on unmount, so a leader with the
  panel open sees a change land without a Reload. Shown to the studio tier
  only (`leadsHere`, the rules' own answer): **the rules were already
  right** — `activityReadAllowed` lets a studio's leaders read their own
  studio's entries and refuses everyone else, and the wave 2 rules test
  covers the leader, the owner, the grant, a trainer and a franchise owner
  with this exact query — so `firestore.rules` and its tests are unchanged
  and nothing needs deploying. A trainer who reads Studio read-only doesn't
  see the panel, because the rules would refuse the read and "couldn't
  read" would be a lie.

## Not built, and what I would build

- **Ask the leader.** Every mention is one clause ("Ask tasks into My
  Studio"; "a setup item sent to the top of a leader's My Studio (it would
  write into Relay's data)"): no record, no screen, no answer to who is
  asked when a studio opening has no leader yet, and no word on what
  happens when the item ticks itself or is skipped. Not safe to guess,
  because it writes into Relay's data. What I would build, for AJ's OK: on
  a studio's setup checklist, **Ask the leader** on an item ticked by a
  person (never on one that ticks itself), which writes ONE `taskRequests`
  document the way a leader's assignment does today (Relay: "trainers
  offer; leaders assign" — an administrator's ask arrives as the leader's,
  with "I can't"), carrying `setupItemId` and the studio, so the Board's
  job and the checklist's item are one thing: ticking either ticks both,
  and a skip closes the job with its reason. Refused in words while the
  studio has no leader named. Recorded in Activity as `assisted-change`.
  Needs: a field on `taskRequests` (a rules change), the Board's card
  saying "From head office", and a talk about whether the leader may hand
  it on.
- **See as.** The round's design flagged it as the refactor it is: the
  studio screens read the app's active studio (`useActiveStudio`), so
  drawing them inside Admins for another studio means every one of them
  takes a studio as an input, or the dashboard swaps the active studio
  (which every listener in the app follows). Read-only first (q9's default)
  doesn't shrink it. What I would build: a **Preview as** door on a studio's
  page that sets the app's pinned studio to it and opens My Studio in the
  ordinary shell (administrators may already enter any studio), with a
  banner "Previewing {studio} — back to Admins" that restores the studio
  they came from. No screen is refactored, every write is the real one (an
  administrator is not bound), and the realm rule holds because Demo Mode
  is never offered.
